# App Store screenshot studio

## Start with one reason to install

Read the product brief, the current listing, the design guide, and the screens in the shipping build. Identify the person, the moment that brings them to the app, the friction they encounter today, and what they want to happen instead. Separate a creative hypothesis from a finding backed by users or conversion data.

For RollReel, the hypothesis is recognition. People already value the clips on their phone. A familiar birthday candle or a friend's laugh can give them a reason to watch again. A filmed moment in the actual player establishes the product faster than a list of playback controls.

## Apply the seven principles

1. **Lead with the value proposition.** Write the first panel so it can introduce the app by itself. Make the product and its main benefit legible at search-result size.
2. **Show an outcome.** Start with something the user can do after installing. For a video library, searching for a birthday clip gives a search field a purpose.
3. **Give each image one message.** Write one sentence describing the job of each panel. Merge two panels if the sentences mean the same thing.
4. **Make the idea visible.** Cover the headline. The subject, action, or selected state should still suggest the promise. A matching search result or a saved thumbnail can do more work than another caption.
5. **Give it a human context.** Pick a specific gesture, face, place, or object that belongs in this product. Use owner-cleared material or original illustrative media. Track its origin and keep customer libraries private.
6. **Earn trust with evidence.** Show the functioning UI. Identify paid features where shown. Qualify privacy claims in terms the product can support. Use ratings, awards, quotations, or figures only when their exact wording, source, date, and usage rights are documented.
7. **Write for a glance.** Use ordinary words and an active verb. Set a short headline large enough to read in the store carousel. If it needs a paragraph to make sense, revise the visual first.

These principles are adapted from the Scrnsht Studio article supplied by the owner. Study the large headline, single focal point, and use of everyday objects in its examples. Give the new work its own words, artwork, type treatment, and composition.

## Produce the images in order

1. Choose five or six outcomes the installed product actually supports. Give the first three slots to the core experience. Introduce a paywall or settings page only when it demonstrates a relevant promise.
2. Storyboard each panel with a headline, exact screen and state, one visual cue, and any paid-access or scope label. Choose an overall reading rhythm. For example, a full player can lead into a close-up of a matching result and then a grid of saved clips.
3. Collect clean captures in the target locale and appearance. Prefer a release build on the target iPhone or its iOS Simulator. Use the same set of cleared media across related screens. Keep a record of capture origin, fixture media, OS, and build. A browser rendering of production widgets is a design proof and needs comparison with the release build before publication.
4. Source the exact hardware model when using a bezel. Use its supplied aperture and offsets, keep its aspect ratio, and preserve the Dynamic Island and safe areas. Retain the original capture through composition. Adding marketing copy outside the device keeps product controls recognizable.
5. Compose one image at a time. Start with a face, clip, selected control, or other evidence. Add the headline, then remove everything that competes with it. Use the app's brand and a restrained type palette. Paid labels and necessary qualifications must survive thumbnail reduction.
6. Export individual numbered, opaque sRGB PNGs and a small contact sheet. Verify dimensions, pixel format, legibility, clipping, spelling, UI state, and sequence. Review every image alone and the first three side by side. Run these checks on the artwork independently of app tests.

## Device and upload checks

Read Apple's current screenshot specifications and check the media slots in the target app's App Store Connect version before delivery. Record orientation, native capture size, export size, and intended display family. A phone frame and the upload canvas are different measurements.

For an **iPhone 16 Pro** portrait composition, the native screen is **1206 × 2622 px** or **402 × 874 points at 3×**. Use a model-specific frame, position the native capture in the frame's aperture, then compose the framed result on the requested canvas. Keep a separate export for any additional required display family. Produce authentic iPad captures when the listing needs iPad screenshots. Record which specifications were verified, which endpoint was unavailable, and whether App Store Connect has accepted the files. [Apple screenshot specifications](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/reference/app-information/screenshot-specifications/) · [App Review Guidelines, Accurate Metadata](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/#accurate-metadata)

## Review the sequence

- Can a first-time visitor describe what the app does after seeing the first image for two seconds?
- Can they still infer the idea with the headline covered?
- Does the first row tell a new part of the story with every swipe?
- Do the captions, media, purchase labels, and actual release build agree?
- Can users in this locale read the headline at approximately 180 px wide? Check useful details at full resolution as well.
- Are the individual PNGs distinct from the contact sheet, and is their upload order unambiguous?

For a conversion experiment, save the existing set, name one change to evaluate, and compare performance for the same storefront and traffic source when enough data is available.
